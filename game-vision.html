Game Vision on TV for Xfinity

A games destination built into the TV: one place to discover, play, watch and connect, all from the sofa. I designed the whole experience from a short product brief, from the first rail of the destination to a detailed page for every game.

Role
Lead UI/UX Designer
Starting point
Product requirements brief
Platform
Xfinity TV (EntOS)
Scope
Destination, Show Center, devices, settings

01 — Overview

Bringing games to the biggest screen in the home.

The industry is gearing up for the next streaming war, and cloud gaming services show that people are ready to play without a console. The opportunity was to make the TV platform the first place people go for games, alongside the shows and films they already watch.

I worked from a short product requirements brief and owned the design end to end: the information architecture, every screen and state, and the motion concepts that make the destination feel alive. The designs were built for EntOS, the entertainment platform shared by Xfinity and Sky, so some screens appear in both brands.

02 — The brief

Four things gamers need to do on TV.

The brief framed the platform’s success around four user needs. Every rail and feature in the destination maps back to at least one of them.

Watch

Watch live gaming content from streaming sources like Twitch.

Connect

Engage with gaming creators, friends and the wider community.

Play

Play cloud games solo or with others, without buying a console.

Control

Easily manage connected devices like controllers and headsets.

  • All in one place
  • Social
  • Safe & trustworthy
  • Personal to me

03 — Who it’s for

From first-time players to family game night.

I used four everyday scenarios to keep the design grounded in real moments in the home.

Scene 1 · A new gamer

Sage tries a game she’s heard about

She spots it promoted on the TV and can play straight away, without investing in an expensive console.

Scene 2 · The community

Theo finds a streamer he recognises

A row of live streamers lets him watch someone play a game he loves without picking up another device.

Scene 3 · Back into battle

Dylan has ten minutes between work and class

Her games are right there. She picks one and jumps back in exactly where she left off.

Scene 4 · Family game night

Mum asks for games the family can play together

The TV suggests camera games that respect the parental controls, so the family can even challenge cousins across town.

04 — Getting in

One tile away, with three ways to say hello.

Games sits in the home screen’s category rail next to Sports, Kids and News, so it feels like a natural part of the TV rather than a separate app. Selecting the tile opens the games destination.

Three destination layouts

The top of the destination can take three forms, so editorial and partner teams can match the layout to the moment, from an everyday line-up to a big launch.

Default

A full-width hero with key art and game details, and rails starting straight underneath.

Billboard

A carousel of large editorial cards, with a peek of the next one to invite browsing.

Premium spotlight

A full-screen takeover for a featured or partner title, made for big releases.

05 — The destination

From the first rail to the last.

I structured the destination as a journey down the page: what’s happening now at the top, your own games next, then wider discovery, community and categories. Each part has a clear job and serves one or more of the four user needs.

  1. 1

    What’s new & trending

    The hero shows a personal pick with the reason why (“Because you played Horizon Zero Dawn”). Below it, Trending now shows what’s popular across the platform, tagged by service.

    • Play
    • Watch
  2. 2

    Your games

    Continue playing brings back games you’ve started, with progress shown on each tile. Games from your playlist collects what you’ve saved for later.

    • Play
  3. 3

    Discover

    A spotlight for a featured game, then curated rails: Best of the year, New games with larger feature tiles, and Coming soon. This is the hook to see everything available.

    • Discover
    • Play
  4. 4

    Connect

    Apps & inputs gives quick access to services like Xbox Game Pass, PlayStation Now, Stadia and GeForce Now. Live now on Twitch surfaces popular streamers with viewer counts, and a camera games rail shows what you can play with the TV camera.

    • Connect
    • Watch
    • Play
  5. 5

    Discover more

    Genre tiles (Action, Role-playing, Puzzle, Kid-friendly) close the page and lead into deeper browsing, so the journey never hits a dead end.

    • Discover

Resume points: jump back in

People shouldn’t have to remember which service each game lives on. Jump back in puts every game in progress in one row, with a progress bar, and opens it right where you left off.

The hero updates as you move along the row, showing the game’s details and how many friends have played it.

06 — Show Center

Everything about a game, on one screen.

Show Center is the game detail page. Using God of War as the example, I designed it to answer every question a player might have before pressing Play, without sending them to their phone.

  1. 1

    Hero

    Key art, the service it’s on, a short synopsis, star rating, genre and age rating. One clear Play button, with quick actions for the trailer, playing with friends, adding to your playlist and more.

  2. 2

    Trailers & screenshots

    See the game in action before committing to it.

  3. 3

    About this game

    The story in short sections, plus the facts in one panel: release date, rating, developer, publisher, genre, formats (4K, HD), accessibility and which services it’s available on. A languages panel shows audio and subtitle support.

  4. 4

    Explore the series

    Every game in the franchise, for fans who want to go back to the start.

  5. 5

    Live channels

    Twitch streamers playing this game right now, with views and timing, connecting the page to the community.

  6. 6

    Critic reviews

    Scores and quotes from outlets like Polygon and IGN, to build confidence before playing.

  7. 7

    Discover more games like this

    Similar games to keep exploring, ending the page with somewhere to go next.

07 — Motion

Making the destination feel alive.

Games are made to be seen in motion, so I explored three ways to bring video into the browsing experience while keeping it calm and in the user’s control.

Hero trailer

After a moment on a game, its trailer starts playing in the hero area behind the details.

Trailers in tiles

A focused tile grows and plays the trailer right in the rail, so people can preview without leaving the page.

Cinemagraph + parallax

Layered key art with subtle movement and depth, made for the billboard and spotlight layouts.

08 — Control & connect

Controllers, friends and peace of mind.

Playing on TV only works if connecting a controller is easy and families feel safe. I designed the supporting features that make that possible.

Friends & devices panel

A side panel that opens over any screen. It shows connected Bluetooth devices with their battery levels, lets you add a new controller or pair an existing one, and lists friends with their online status.

Hardware in quick settings

Controllers and audio output sit in the quick settings overlay, so players can switch headsets or check a controller mid-game without leaving it.

Settings, privacy & subscriptions

Parents can set age restrictions and privacy controls, and everyone can see their active subscriptions, like Xbox Game Pass, with renewal dates, alongside a full manage devices screen.

Live cam gaming integration

Using the TV’s camera, players can stream their video feed to friends while they play, or play camera games together from different homes.

09 — Outcome

A vision the business could rally around.

The work became the design vision for games on the platform. Comcast and Sky teams collaborated on it as the main deck for senior leadership. It gave stakeholders something concrete to react to: a full destination, a detailed game page and the supporting features, rather than a list of requirements.

The biggest challenge was balancing a lot of content with the calm, lean-back feel of TV. Grouping rails by purpose, keeping one clear action per screen and using motion with restraint kept the experience easy to navigate with a remote.
